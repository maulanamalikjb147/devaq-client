Lump charcoal and shaped briquettes

Lump wood charcoal retains the grain and irregular shape of the original wood. A buyer working with an open grate may prefer larger pieces and specify how much undersized material a sack can contain.

Briquettes are made by pressing charcoal particles into a defined shape. A cube gives a distributor a repeatable unit to count or pack. For a retail barbecue bag, the weight of a pillow briquette and the number of broken pieces can be more useful points of comparison.

Further reading: FAO on charcoal briquetting.

How to read a charcoal sample

Spread a representative portion on a clean tray. Measure several pieces, photograph them next to a ruler, then weigh the small fragments left at the bottom of the pack. Keep the sample code in the photo so another reviewer can identify it.

Moisture adds to the delivered weight. Ash is the mineral residue left after combustion. When you compare lab results, record whether the supplier reports each figure on a dry basis or as received. Ask for the test method and the lot that the report covers.

Further reading: FAO on charcoal quality control.

Check the pack as well as the fuel

Agree the net weight, inner pack count and acceptable breakage before the order. A 10 kg carton of small boxes serves a different purpose from a 10 kg bulk sack, even when the quoted price per kilogram is similar.

On delivery, record damaged sacks or crushed cartons before opening them. Compare the label with the approved sample and keep a photograph of the lot reference with your receiving notes.
