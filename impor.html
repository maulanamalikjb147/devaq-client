International purchasing

Import sourcing.

PT You Company also purchases goods from overseas. A workable import brief links the item and its specification to a packing plan, a handover point and a receiving date.

On this page

Define the goods before asking for a rate

A useful brief identifies the item, how it will be used and which dimensions or materials must match. Include a drawing or photograph when a supplier needs to see how the part will fit. Separate fixed requirements from preferences such as carton count or label placement.

For trial orders, describe the test the buyer expects to run. A supplier can then quote a sample in the right format and identify any difference between the sample and the proposed production lot.

Example purchase: tea filter paper

This fictional purchase illustrates a packaging inquiry for a tea-packing line. The buyer wants to check reel fit and sealing behavior before ordering a production quantity.

Item
Heat-sealable tea filter paper in reels
Working specification
125 mm web width, 76 mm core, 18 g/m² basis weight
Trial
One short reel for the buyer’s packing line
Illustrative MOQ
40 reels of 5 kg each, totaling 200 kg net
Export packing
4 reels per carton, 10 cartons per example order
Requested handover
Delivered to the buyer’s warehouse in Jakarta, with freight itemized

Ask the supplier for a dimensioned reel drawing, storage guidance and documentation for the intended application. Record the machine settings used in the trial so the purchasing team can identify what the sample approval actually covers.

Compare like-for-like quotations

Record currency, unit price, quote validity and the named delivery point. Ask which freight and handling charges are included. Distinguish the date the goods will be ready at the supplier from the estimated arrival at the destination.

In the filter paper example, a price per reel becomes comparable only after both suppliers state the net paper weight per reel. If one offer uses a heavier reel, convert each offer to the same price per net kilogram before comparing freight.

Build a shipment file

Give the buyer, supplier and forwarder the same purchase reference. Before booking, agree on the named handover point, who arranges carriage, and which party will send each document to the receiver.

Commercial invoice
Match the goods description, quantity, currency and agreed price to the order.
Packing list
Reconcile net and gross weights, carton counts and lot marks with the actual packed goods.
Freight record
Confirm the booking reference, transport document, consignee details and expected arrival with the forwarder.
Lot evidence
Keep the approved specifications, inspection record and loading photographs together. Confirm any destination-specific documents with the importer before dispatch.

Match the shipment to the order

Give the receiving team the approved specification, sample reference and packing list. At arrival, count the outer packs and record visible damage before opening them. Then check the contents against the item code and the agreed net weight.

For the example order, ten cartons should contain forty reels. If one carton is short, record its label and the quantity actually received. Keeping photographs with the purchase reference gives the supplier a specific discrepancy to investigate.
